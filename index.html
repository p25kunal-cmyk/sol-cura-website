There are two main types of sunscreen — mineral and chemical. Understanding each helps you choose the best sunscreen for oily skin, brown skin, sensitive skin, or any lifestyle.

Feature Mineral (Sol Cura) Chemical SPF
How it works ✓ Sits on skin, reflects UV rays Absorbs into skin, converts UV to heat
Key active ingredient ✓ Zinc oxide Oxybenzone, avobenzone, octinoxate
Skin sensitivity ✓ Gentler — ideal for sensitive skin Can irritate reactive or sensitive skin
White cast potential ✓ Minimised in Sol Cura formula Generally no white cast
Reef considerations ✓ No oxybenzone or octinoxate ✗ Often contains reef-harmful filters
Works immediately ✓ Effective on application Requires ~15 min absorption time

FAQ

Common Questions, Honest Answers.

Mineral sunscreen uses physical UV-blocking ingredients — primarily zinc oxide — to create a barrier on the surface of your skin that reflects and scatters UV rays. Unlike chemical sunscreens, which absorb into the skin and convert UV radiation into heat, mineral sunscreen sits on top of the skin. This makes it a popular choice for sensitive skin and those who prefer a more environmentally considered formula.
Sol Cura is formulated without oxybenzone and octinoxate — two chemical UV filters that have been linked to coral reef damage and are banned in certain protected marine environments. Our zinc oxide-based formula is a reef-conscious alternative to conventional chemical sunscreens.
Traditional mineral sunscreens can leave a visible white cast — especially on medium to deeper skin tones. Sol Cura is specifically formulated to minimise this. Our zinc oxide-based formula is designed to blend to a sheer, lightweight finish across a range of skin tones. While individual results may vary, minimising white cast is a core priority of our formulation.

Sol Cura is formulated with a lightweight, non-greasy finish that works well for oily and combination skin types. The mineral zinc oxide formula helps provide a clean, non-occlusive feel that many people with oily skin find more comfortable than thicker chemical sunscreen formulas.

There are two main types of sunscreen: Mineral sunscreen (physical sunscreen) uses zinc oxide to sit on the skin and physically reflect UV rays. Using a lightweight sunscreen consistently every day is widely considered the best first step for managing dark spots.
